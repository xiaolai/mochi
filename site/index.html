One element

No build step, no framework. The tag above this line is the real one — it makes its own canvas, sizes it at device resolution, runs the frame loop, and stops when it leaves the document. 

<script type="module">
  import 'https://esm.sh/mochi-avatar/element'
</script>

<dough-avatar emotion="happy"></dough-avatar>

Or any 2D context

import { DoughAvatar, MOCHI } from 'mochi-avatar'

const her = new DoughAvatar(canvas.getContext('2d'), { face: MOCHI, size: 'fit-canvas' })
her.resize(300, 300, devicePixelRatio)
her.setEmotion({ emotion: 'happy', intensity: 1 })

Browser, worker, Node, Electron — CanvasRenderingContext2D is used structurally, so nothing needs an adapter. Zero runtime dependencies.

She is alive before you tell her anything

Breath on a 3400ms one-sided curve. Blinks drawn from a clamped exponential, because blinking is a Poisson process and a uniform gap reads as a metronome within about thirty seconds. A few pixels of drift, from three incommensurate sines that never visibly repeat. An idle character holding perfectly still reads as a crashed one.

Eight expressions

neutral · happy · shy · sad · angry · surprised · thinking · sleepy

Multipliers on the neutral geometry rather than separate artwork, so one silhouette carries all eight. Sleepy has no mouth on purpose — a mouth left on a sleeping face reads as awake-but-quiet.

Six colourways

matcha · sakura · kinako · yuzu · ramune · budo

One geometry throughout; five colour fields swapped. Measured on the sprite exports, the six have pixel-identical alpha channels — she is the shape, not the colour.

The desktop app has been retired

Mochi began as a realtime-voice companion that lived on a macOS desktop. That app is no longer developed, and the character outlived it — which is the usual way round.

The downloads still work. v0.1.20 remains available, signed and notarized for Apple silicon and Intel, and its source is preserved on the archive/app branch exactly as it shipped.

Every picture on this page was rendered by the engine that ships, from her own constants — never drawn by eye. A likeness drawn by hand looks right on the day and stops matching the product the first time somebody tunes a shoulder exponent.
